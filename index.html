Manifesto Machines

20 music-making tools. Each one turns a real artist's manifesto into the rules of a music system, and is designed as a homage to a graphic designer or design style. Open one, press PLAY (it plays built-in sounds straight away), drop in your own samples (WAV, AIFF, MP3), record to WAV, and press LEARN to read about the manifesto and the design.

Every tool is one standalone HTML file that works offline from your disk in Chrome or Edge. Source on GitHub · Ledger

01

Accelerator

Manifesto The Founding and Manifesto of Futurism (Filippo Tommaso Marinetti, 1909)
Design Plakatstil (German 'poster style')

A drum machine that can only speed up, until it crashes and starts again.
02

Intonarumori

Manifesto The Art of Noises (L'arte dei rumori) (Luigi Russolo, 1913)
Design Neville Brody

Six noise machines, one for each of Russolo's six families of noise, cranked by hand.
03

Enharmonic

Manifesto Manifesto of Futurist Musicians (Francesco Balilla Pratella, 1910)
Design Fortunato Depero

A quarter-tone melody machine in free metre, where every bar chooses its own length.
04

Cut-up Hat

Manifesto Dada Manifesto 1918 (Tristan Tzara, 1918)
Design David Carson

Your loop is cut into words, shaken in a hat and played back in the order the pieces come out.
05

Automatic

Manifesto Manifesto of Surrealism (André Breton, 1924)
Design Tadanori Yokoo

Draw without stopping to think: your hand becomes a melody and an exquisite corpse in three parts.
06

Neo-Plastic Grid

Manifesto De Stijl Manifesto I (Theo van Doesburg, Piet Mondrian, Vilmos Huszár, Antony Kok and others, 1918)
Design Wim Crouwel

A Mondrian composition you can hear: vertical lines are time, horizontal lines are pitch, colours are instruments.
07

Building

Manifesto Programme of the Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar (Walter Gropius, 1919)
Design Herbert Bayer

Build a song floor by floor from Bauhaus shapes: blue circles, red squares, yellow triangles.
08

Kinetic Rhythms

Manifesto The Realistic Manifesto (Naum Gabo and Antoine Pevsner, 1920)
Design Gustav Klutsis

Three rotating rods sweep through points in space; every crossing is a note, so rhythm comes from motion, not mass.
09

Zero of Form

Manifesto From Cubism and Futurism to Suprematism: The New Painterly Realism (Kazimir Malevich, 1915)
Design El Lissitzky

Pure shapes float on white; each one is a held tone, and where they overlap your sample sparkles.
10

Zaum

Manifesto A Slap in the Face of Public Taste (David Burliuk, Aleksei Kruchenykh, Vladimir Mayakovsky and Velimir Khlebnikov, 1912)
Design Alexander Rodchenko

Type words that mean nothing in any language and a throat made of your sample sings them.
11

Theory of Intervals

Manifesto WE: Variant of a Manifesto (Dziga Vertov (the Kinoks group), 1922)
Design Vladimir and Georgii Stenberg

A film strip of sample 'shots', where the loudest music happens in the cuts between them.
12

BLAST / BLESS

Manifesto BLAST, Manifesto of the Vorticists (Wyndham Lewis, with Ezra Pound, Gaudier-Brzeska and others, 1914)
Design Paula Scher

Every instrument is a word on the wall: BLAST it and it hits hard and dirty, BLESS it and it turns soft and holy.
13

Event Scores

Manifesto Fluxus Manifesto (George Maciunas, 1963)
Design Experimental Jetset

A deck of Fluxus-style instruction cards; the machine performs whichever card is on top, then flips the next.
14

Dérive

Manifesto Report on the Construction of Situations (Guy Debord, 1957)
Design Jamie Reid

A walker drifts through a psychogeographic city; each district has its own sound, and the city's pull decides the route.
15

Painted Lines

Manifesto The Stuckists manifesto (Billy Childish and Charles Thomson, 1999)
Design Corita Kent

Paint melodies by hand in screen-print layers; the loop prints one colour at a time, then all of them together.
16

Vow of Chastity

Manifesto Dogme 95 Manifesto and Vow of Chastity (Lars von Trier and Thomas Vinterberg, 1995)
Design Brutalist web design

One sample, no effects, no grid: you play it live by hand, and every rule you break goes in a public confession.
17

Credo

Manifesto The Future of Music: Credo (John Cage, 1937 (a talk in Seattle; published 1958))
Design Ikko Tanaka

Three coins tossed six times build an I Ching hexagram, and the hexagram picks the sound, its length and its loudness from a chart.
18

Breath Squares

Manifesto Sonic Meditations (Pauline Oliveros, 1971)
Design Josef Albers

Four nested squares are four drones that breathe; colours that sit close together make their tones beat against each other.
19

BECAUSE

Manifesto Riot Grrrl Manifesto (Kathleen Hanna (with Bikini Kill), 1991)
Design Barbara Kruger

A three-chord punk machine where your BECAUSE lines become the riffs and the shouted rhythm.
20

Cyborg

Manifesto A Cyborg Manifesto (Donna Haraway, 1985)
Design April Greiman

Three boundary sliders (human/animal, organism/machine, physical/non-physical) blur one groove between flesh and code.
